# Educational Architecture

## Core Mental Model

The product teaches a loop:

1. Notice the return moment.
2. Name the story that made smoking feel useful again.
3. Separate nicotine discomfort from the meaning attached to it.
4. Choose one small recovery move.
5. Re-enter freedom today, not after a perfect streak.

## Learning Modules

### 1. The Return Moment

Purpose: replace shame with observation.

Key idea: relapse is most useful when it is specific. "I smoked" is too broad. "I smoked after a tense call, alone, while thinking I deserved relief" is usable.

### 2. The False Benefit

Purpose: identify what smoking appeared to offer.

Possible false benefits:
- relief;
- reward;
- pause;
- identity;
- rebellion;
- focus;
- companionship;
- closure.

### 3. The Fear Hook

Purpose: expose the fear that makes stopping feel like loss.

Common hooks:
- "I will be deprived."
- "I cannot handle stress without it."
- "I already failed, so today is ruined."
- "It was only one, so it does not count."
- "I need a perfect restart moment."

### 4. The Practical Friction

Purpose: make the next risky moment harder to repeat.

Examples:
- remove the easiest access point;
- change the first 10 minutes after meals;
- create a response for social pressure;
- choose one replacement pause that is not framed as sacrifice.

### 5. The Return Plan

Purpose: convert insight into a short plan.

Output:
- one sentence of clarity;
- one risk moment;
- one micro-action;
- one support option;
- one return reminder.

## Source Notes

- Evidence-backed: triggers, cravings, withdrawal, quit planning, support options.
- Expert-inspired: false benefit / fear / sacrifice language.
- Product assumption: shorter modules are better than a long educational wall for relapse recovery.

## Become A Student Track

The app now includes a reading track for deeper self-study. This track is Allen Carr-inspired, not official or copied, and focuses on re-learning the mechanism after relapse.

Initial lessons:
- relapse does not erase insight;
- cigarette as promise, not real pleasure;
- fear of loss;
- "just one" as permission story;
- stress and the illusion of help;
- the pause without the cigarette;
- non-smoker identity after relapse;
- shame as loop extender;
- preparing the next trigger;
- returning as daily practice.

Each lesson should include:
- one main idea;
- a generous reading block;
- one reflection question;
- one micro-exercise.

Source strength: expert-inspired and product assumption. Keep the medical boundary visible elsewhere in the app.
